Both carry the same guardrails, because the risk with a generated narrative is not that it says too little — it is that it states something provably wrong with total confidence in a document that goes to the board. The rules travel with the data:

  • Every number you use must be quoted from the digest or the embedded model. Do not compute new figures and present them as measurements.
  • Never quote committedPoints, committedIssues or carriedOut for a CLOSED sprint. They count every issue in that sprint today, so an item that passed through sixteen sprints counts as committed in all sixteen. They are accurate for the ACTIVE sprint only.
  • Never compare story points between teams. Estimation culture differs per board; a point on one board is not a point on another.
  • Never build a per-person productivity ranking. The assignee field records who holds a ticket today, not who did the work.
  • A null means not measurable. It never means zero.
  • Lead time is created-to-resolved and includes backlog dwell. Do not call it cycle time.
  • Review rates are over merge requests a PERSON opened. Bot-authored merge requests are excluded and counted separately; including them roughly quadruples the apparent unreviewed rate and describes the robots, not the team.
  • Where a figure has a caveat in the digest, carry the caveat into the sentence. Do not strip it to make the sentence cleaner.

What a “team” means here, and what the numbers are measured against
A team is a Jira board
Not the Jira Team field, which is empty on every issue on this site, and not the project, which is too coarse — one project can host several boards, and one board can carry several projects' tickets.
There is no target anywhere in this report
Every expectation is the team's own recent history: the p10, p50 and p90 of what it has actually completed in its last dozen closed sprints. Nobody has to agree to a number for the comparison to be fair, and “you are committed above your own best-ever sprint” is a sentence that survives being argued with.
Story points are never compared between teams
Estimation culture differs wildly by board here — one board leaves 97% of its active sprint unestimated, another's median sprint is 51 points against a third's 28. A point on one board is not a point on another, and this report never adds them together.
There is deliberately no per-person productivity figure
Assignee is on every issue, so it could be computed. It would not survive being questioned: points measure the estimate rather than the difficulty or the value, and the person who spends a day unblocking two colleagues scores zero. What is measured instead is practice — who merges work with nobody looking at it, and who reviews other people's. Both are habits a conversation can change.
Colour
healthy · worth watching · needs you · not measurable. “Not measurable” is never drawn as zero: a burndown from one observation is a flat line, and a flat line is the picture of a sprint going perfectly.

Glossary

Story point
A relative size the team put on a ticket. It measures the estimate, not the hours and not the value.
p10 / p50 / p90
Percentiles of what this team completed in its own recent closed sprints. p50 is the median — a typical sprint. p90 is close to its best ever. p10 is what to promise when a date must be kept.
Spread
p90 minus p10, as a share of the median. Above 100% the band is wider than the median itself, so the median is not a planning number.
Carryover
Work that has been in three or more sprints. Almost always means the item is too big or too vague, not that it is being worked badly.
WIP
Work in progress — how much is started but not finished, at once. Parallel work does not finish faster; it finishes later, all at once.
Cycle time
From the first day work was seen in progress to the day it was resolved. Excludes backlog dwell.
Lead time
From created to resolved. Includes however long it sat in the backlog, which is usually most of it.
Scope churn
Work added to, removed from, or re-estimated inside a sprint after it started. A 3 that becomes an 8 moved the commitment by 5 points without a ticket moving.
Merged unreviewed
A merge request that went in with no comment and no approval from anybody but its author. Counted only over merge requests a person opened.
Epic
A larger piece of work several tickets belong to. The level at which “when will this be done” has an answer.
Flagged
Jira's formal “this is blocked” marker. This report also looks for blocker language in comments, because most teams write it there and forget the flag.

Why there is no points-per-person figure anywhere in this report. Assignee is on every issue, so it could be computed — but it would not survive being questioned. Estimation culture differs wildly by board, points measure the estimate rather than the difficulty or the value, and the person who spends a day unblocking two colleagues scores zero. What is measured instead is practice: who merges work with nobody looking at it, and who reviews other people's. Both are habits training can change, and neither ranks anyone at their job.
